# Strategic power audit (`strategic-power-audit`)

- **Phenomenon / range / input:** A focused claim that a current or proposed condition could support persistent differential returns for a defined business in a defined market. Use it only after the audit direction, minimum customer job, evidence state, and materially different rival response paths are explicit.
- **Why use it:** Strategy talk often calls any advantage a moat. This operation forces two separate demonstrations: a material benefit and a mechanism that prevents capable rivals from arbitraging it away.
- **Procedure:**
  0. Classify readiness before preparing the ledger. Confirm whether the audit concerns **current Power**, **proposed future Power**, or **Power potential**; the exact business, market, period, claim, and minimum customer job; at least one source beyond aspiration for Benefit; at least one observed mechanism for the Barrier; rival evidence covering the main neutralization routes; a negative control; contrary evidence; and known gaps. Return **Ready**, **Provisional**, or **Not runnable**. A Provisional run may return a hypothesis ledger and evidence requirements but no firm Power classification. If Not runnable, stop with the missing framing or evidence.
  1. Freeze the confirmed case. Define any load-bearing term whose meanings differ across products, such as branching, reactive, workflow, or network. State the minimum customer outcome before comparing implementations. Do not switch between product, company, or ecosystem levels midway. If the direction changes materially after preparation, stop and ask whether the user wants a new run under the corrected claim.
  2. When the claim is compound, decompose its parts and state the interaction mechanism. Permit A + B + C only when the interaction itself changes Benefit or Barrier; do not add several weak scores into one strong claim.
  3. Trace **use value** first: product condition → user or agent capability → observed or proposed behavior. Keep observation, source claim, and hypothesis distinct.
  4. Trace **economic capture** separately: behavior → selection, willingness to pay, cost, investment, retention, and cash flow. State the claimed Benefit as higher price, lower cost, or lower required investment relative to a capable rival. Quantify it when possible; otherwise mark the missing measurement.
  5. State the claimed **Barrier**: who is blocked, by what, for how long, and under which market conditions. Treat technical difficulty as a barrier candidate only. A supported Barrier needs evidence of capable-rival inability, unacceptable economics, structural conflict, or late arrival.
  6. Separate current exclusion from renewal. A dynamic durability claim needs evidence about learning rate, release rate, accumulated process, response cycles, or another repeatable renewal mechanism. The ability to keep innovating is not evidence by itself.
  7. Build a rival-coverage grid across materially different responses: direct copying, same-job substitution through another architecture, distribution or platform control, and price. Use the strongest supported rivals with comparable resources, competence, incentive, and time. Ask whether each can neutralize the customer outcome without copying the product.
  8. Optionally classify a supported mechanism as scale, network, counter-positioning, switching, brand, cornered resource, or embedded process. Treat the label as an index, never as evidence.
  9. Freeze a negative control: an ordinary execution or product advantage that creates value but lacks a blocking mechanism. Confirm that the audit returns **advantage without Power** for it.
  10. Record readiness, Benefit, Barrier, renewal, and the whole claim separately as supported, unsupported, or indeterminate. Claim Power only when readiness is Ready, Benefit and Barrier are supported, and the materially different rival responses remain blocked.
- **Readout / control:** A Power ledger containing readiness, audit type, defined terms, use-value chain, economic-capture chain, compound interaction when present, Benefit mechanism and magnitude, Barrier mechanism, renewal evidence, rival-coverage grid, duration, contrary evidence, gaps, and separate statuses. Valid whole-claim outcomes are `supported Power`, `advantage without Power`, `barrier without demonstrated benefit`, `unsupported`, and `indeterminate`; a Provisional corpus may return only a hypothesis ledger and research gap.
- **Common artifacts:** Naming a category instead of testing it; answering a weaker claim than the user's; moving from technical inputs straight to cash flow; counting awareness as brand power; treating any flywheel as a network effect; adding weak component claims; comparing one friendly rival; treating technical difficulty or current lead time as durable exclusion; claiming renewal from generic innovation ability.
- **Escalate / stop:** Stop when audit type, market, economic unit, minimum customer job, or material rival response paths cannot be defined; when the corpus is Not runnable; or when the direction changes after preparation. Escalate to an ecosystem dependency blueprint when the benefit depends on independent complementors, or to a real-world check when a reversible market test could measure the benefit. Do not run those instruments or recommend a strategic move without explicit selection or request.
- **What it requires:** Market and competitor evidence, behavioral or financial proxies, a declared horizon, a negative control, rival-relative evidence, and disciplined handling of unknowns. A Provisional reading must keep its evidence requirements beside the result.
- **Execution placement:** **Either.** The orchestrator may run the ledger with the user. A fresh subagent can improve the capable-rival counterfactual when confirmation bias is material, but it must receive the frozen claim and evidence rather than the desired classification. The orchestrator returns the bounded ledger.
- **Donor basis:** Hamilton Helmer's Benefit × Barrier definition of Power. The transfer preserves the cash-flow and capable-rival tests and refuses to treat the seven labels as a checklist.
