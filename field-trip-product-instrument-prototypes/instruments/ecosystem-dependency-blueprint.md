# Ecosystem dependency blueprint (`ecosystem-dependency-blueprint`)

- **Phenomenon / range / input:** Multilateral dependence among independently deciding actors required to deliver one bounded, user-confirmed value proposition. The instrument seeks failure hidden outside the focal organization's own execution.
- **Why use it:** A product can work and still fail because another innovation arrives late, an intermediary will not adopt, or one actor receives too little value to participate. Ordinary value chains often hide those independent decisions.
- **Procedure:**
  0. Make a readiness record before drawing the blueprint. Confirm the intended question, end-user outcome, system boundary, focal organization, time horizon, material actor classes, control boundaries, known missing perspectives, and evidence for at least one material independent decision. Ask the user to confirm the direction. Return **Ready**, **Provisional**, or **Not runnable**. A Provisional run may return a dependency hypothesis and evidence gaps but no firm broken-link or leverage claim. If the outcome, boundary, or necessary independent decision cannot be fixed, stop as Not runnable.
  1. Freeze the confirmed end-user value proposition in outcome terms. Do not begin from the focal firm's org chart, product boundary, or preferred partners. If the outcome or system boundary changes materially after preparation, stop and ask whether the user wants a new run under the corrected boundary.
  2. Enumerate every activity required for the end user to receive that value. Include integration, distribution, migration, certification, operation, support, and other enabling work when necessary.
  3. Assign each activity to an actor and mark whether the focal organization controls that actor. Record roles, positions, handoffs, unresolved assignments, and the evidence source for each material actor claim.
  4. Challenge coverage: ask who could veto, delay, reroute, or refuse delivery but is absent from the map, and whether a substitute chain could deliver the same outcome. Preserve omitted actors and alternate chains rather than forcing closure.
  5. Draw the minimum dependency structure that links the value proposition to all required activities. Distinguish sequence, exchange, and mutual dependence.
  6. Mark **co-innovation dependencies** where another innovation must become technically and commercially ready. For each, record ability, willingness, timing, and evidence.
  7. Mark **adoption-chain dependencies** where an intermediary must choose to adopt before the end user can receive value. For each necessary adopter, record incremental benefit, cost, switching burden, outside option, and resulting surplus or unknown.
  8. Calculate joint success only when the required events and their probabilities are defensible. Multiply independent probabilities; use conditional branches for dependent risks. Otherwise return an ordinal dependency record without false precision.
  9. Freeze a negative control by redrawing the case as an internally controlled delivery chain. If no material independent decision remains, stop with `not an ecosystem dependency case`.
  10. Return readiness, the blueprint, coverage gaps, and the first broken or unknown link. Name the dependency whose change would alter delivery conditions most only when readiness is Ready and the evidence distinguishes it. Do not choose partners or prescribe incentives unless the user separately asks.
- **Readout / control:** A readiness record, value-proposition anchor, actor/activity table, coverage challenge, dependency graph, co-innovation register, adoption-chain surplus register, timing conflicts, evidence gaps, and bounded broken-link reading. The control is the internally controlled chain and each actor's individual participation test; aggregate ecosystem benefit cannot compensate a necessary actor with negative surplus.
- **Common artifacts:** Treating any industry as an ecosystem; mapping from the focal firm's view; omitting low-status intermediaries or veto actors; assuming shared goals; using end-user value as evidence of partner willingness; multiplying correlated probabilities; treating a partnership announcement as operational readiness; averaging surplus across actors.
- **Escalate / stop:** Stop when readiness is Not runnable, the outcome or boundary changes after preparation, or necessary activities cannot be stated. Return only a hypothesis blueprint and evidence gaps when readiness is Provisional. Escalate to a strategic power audit when the question becomes who captures persistent returns, or to a real-world check when one safe partner commitment could test a link. The blueprint does not decide ecosystem design.
- **What it requires:** Evidence from several actor positions, explicit unknowns, a fixed value-proposition boundary, and care with probability dependence. Research outside supplied material requires separate authority.
- **Execution placement:** **Orchestrator.** The user may correct roles, value, willingness, and control boundaries as the map forms. Fresh actor-specific research can be added only when authorized and must remain source-labeled. The orchestrator returns one bounded blueprint rather than synthesizing an ecosystem strategy.
- **Donor basis:** Ron Adner's value blueprint, co-innovation risk, adoption-chain risk, and broken-link test. The transfer preserves independently minded actors and actor-level surplus as the defining controls.
