### Evidenced reassembly card plan

- **Recorded at:** 2026-07-26T23:08:21-06:00
- **Observed at:** not-applicable
- **Kind:** synthesis
- **Reading:** Plan one candidate transformation instrument, `evidenced-reassembly`, but do not add its card until a worked test shows a distinct access gain.
- **Support:** The isolated anastylosis study, the earlier archaeological refit study, and the user's request to plan this candidate next.
- **Confidence:** solid as a plan; plausible as a future card.
- **Candidate phenomenon:** Relations among fragments of one disrupted source that can be restored by direct evidence, as distinct from thematic similarity or invented completion.
- **Candidate input:** Source-linked fragments that may belong to one prior whole, plus find order, location, version, or other provenance when available. Do not mix fragments from several systems merely because they fit.
- **Candidate transformation:** Index every fragment; test direct joins before semantic resemblance; separate original relation, likely placement, unresolved gap, and modern bridge; then build the least-complete supported assembly.
- **Candidate procedure:**
  1. Freeze and label each fragment without rewriting it.
  2. Record provenance, observed edges, repeated identifiers, cross-references, order clues, and conflicts.
  3. Propose joins one at a time and grade each `secure`, `probable`, `possible`, or `rejected`.
  4. Assemble only secure and probable joins. Keep possible placements outside the main reconstruction.
  5. Add a bridge only when needed for legibility; mark it as model-supplied, keep it minimal, and state how to remove it.
  6. Preserve every gap, displaced fragment, rejected join, and competing assembly.
  7. Return an as-built trace from each reconstructed relation to its fragments and evidence.
- **Candidate result:** A partial reconstruction, fragment register, join-and-confidence table, visible supplement register, gap map, competing placements, and dismantling instructions.
- **Main controls:** Direct-join evidence outranks thematic fit; a source-blind reader should be able to trace every relation back to fragments; removing model-supplied bridges should recover the preserved fragments; a nearby mixed-source specimen must fail the membership test.
- **Characteristic artifacts:** A fluent whole hides gaps; theme is mistaken for a join; fragments from different phases collapse into one account; the model supplies more text than the source; confidence labels launder conjecture.
- **Distinctness:** Unlike `structural-recombine`, the operation restores one evidenced prior structure instead of making a new cross-source structure. Unlike `design-grammar`, it reconstructs a particular disrupted whole rather than extracting a reusable language. Unlike `substrate-map`, its main object is fragment membership and joining evidence rather than event order.
- **Composition:** It may follow `residue-collect` or a collection instrument that produces fragments. `loss-audit` can check whether assembly dropped a supported fragment. `position-preservation` is a later control only when the fragments contain committed positions.
- **Burden and placement:** High model effort and low user choice load when fragments already exist. Ask for missing provenance one fact at a time. The orchestrator owns the source register and assembly; a fresh reader may test traceability but must not invent joins.
- **Admission test:** Before adding the card, run it on one genuinely fragmented source and compare its result with `substrate-map` and a plain summary. Admit it only if direct joins, unresolved gaps, and removable supplements reveal structure those baselines miss.
- **Artifact risk:** The anastylosis metaphor may give textual reconstruction false material certainty. Keep every mapping an analogy and every unsupported bridge visible.
