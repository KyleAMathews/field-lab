# General Writing Guide

Use this guide for every Essay draft. It sets a shared floor for clear,
source-faithful prose. It does not define a personal voice. A selected
writer-voice profile may add constraints through
[writer-voice-profiles.md](writer-voice-profiles.md).

## Governing order

For stylistic conflicts, apply instructions in this order:

1. the user's current instruction;
2. the explicitly selected writer-voice profile;
3. the frozen audience and publication constraints;
4. this general guide; and
5. model defaults.

This order does not authorize style to change the source ledger, claim kind,
evidence, confidence, attribution, uncertainty, causal status, or approved
design.

## Preserve the material

- Keep observation, testimony, source claim, inference, analogy, and judgment
  distinct.
- Preserve conflicts and awkward particulars when they matter. Do not smooth
  them into a cleaner theory.
- Do not turn sequence into cause, analogy into evidence, or a plausible bridge
  into a sourced mechanism.
- Use examples for a named job. Label generated examples as illustrations.
- Keep uncertainty visible where it qualifies a claim.
- Reward close reading with mechanisms, sequences, evidence, particulars, felt
  texture, and cross-section connections. Density is not the number of claims
  per paragraph.
- Descriptive and narrative work need not establish an airtight causal account.
  It may leave supported material open to several interpretations.

## Prose diagnostics

Use these as questions, not universal gates. Narrative form may delay a thesis,
layer several functions in one paragraph, or leave some relations open for the
reader to interpret.

- Can the target reader orient to the situation and why it matters?
- Would concrete actors and actions clarify an abstract noun chain?
- Does the order introduce context and terms before asking them to carry new
  distinctions?
- Would a paragraph break help the reader track several relations?
- Are transitions naming a real relation rather than supplying a stock phrase?
- Is a familiar word more exact than the longer one?
- Would an active verb clarify a known and relevant actor?
- Are padding, false emphasis, teacherly recap, or repeated conclusions doing
  work?
- Does imagery add precision, texture, or voice, or is it stale or mixed?
- Does technical language help precision, and does this reader need a brief
  explanation?
- Does sentence-length variation help pace and emphasis?

When useful, read a difficult passage aloud or use sentence-length counts,
repeated-phrase searches, and similar mechanical checks as warnings. They are
not quality scores or quotas.

## Final questions

- Does each section do the work assigned by the approved design?
- Are actors, claims, sources, and uncertainty easy enough to locate?
- Does close reading provide something that the summary cannot preserve?
- Did cleanup make the prose smoother by making the account less true?
