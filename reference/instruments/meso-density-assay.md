# Meso-Level Density Assay (`meso-density-assay`)

- **Phenomenon sought:** Text-supported differences, if any, between what a
  short reconstruction carries and what a careful encounter with the full draft
  makes available.
- **Why use it:** A summary check can show whether readers recover the headline,
  but not whether mechanisms, sequences, evidence, particulars, tensions, felt
  texture, or connections survive compression. The assay also permits the null
  result that close reading adds no verified function beyond more detail.
- **Operating range:** Use on a complete frozen draft or a substantial section.
  It tests recoverable close-reading difference, not length, literary merit,
  novelty, or whether the essay should exist.
- **Input:** Frozen draft or section, frozen compression target, one fresh
  summary reader, one separate fresh close reader, exact neutral prompts, and
  access to the text for verification.
- **What changes:** The first reading compresses the text. The second compares
  the full text with that summary and reports what, if anything, compression
  loses.

## Procedure

1. Freeze the exact text and compression target. Use 100 words as the default
   for a full essay; choose and record a shorter target for a section. Do not
   give either reader the intended thesis, reader promise, validation history,
   author-value account, or desired answer.
2. Give the text to one fresh reader and ask for an accurate summary at the
   frozen length. Preserve the exact response.
3. Check only whether the response is usable: it addresses the supplied text
   and attempts the requested compression. Preserve inaccuracies as calibration.
   If the response is unusable, preserve it and permit one fresh replacement;
   if that also fails, downgrade or stop.
4. Give the text and usable summary to a separate fresh reader. Ask what, if
   anything, close reading provides that the summary cannot, and where the text
   provides it.
5. Keep the close reader blind to the author's account of value, preferred
   verdict, and other reader results.
6. Verify every claimed difference against the text. Classify supported
   differences by function, such as mechanism, sequence, evidence, particular,
   tension, cross-section connection, or felt texture.
7. Separate functional difference, mere extra detail, unsupported reader
   inference, material an accurate summary should have retained, and a null
   result.
8. Return the comparison without scoring the essay or choosing a revision.

- **Result:** Text pointer, compression target, summary attempts, close-reading
  comparison, verified locations, difference classifications, unsupported
  inferences, disagreements, null result when present, and sensitivity limits.
- **Control:** Frozen text and target, neutral prompts, separate fresh contexts,
  and claim-by-claim verification. Fresh model contexts remain correlated and
  do not represent a reader population.
- **Common distortions:** The summary reader writes an inaccurate summary; an
  unusable response is retried until a preferred summary appears; the close
  reader rewards sheer quantity; vague praise replaces text locations; ornate
  phrasing is mistaken for felt texture; or author intent enters the prompts.
- **Escalate / stop:** Use an actual target reader when audience or publication
  stakes require it. Report a mismatch with the approved design without choosing
  whether to revise, accept the essay's scale, or return to design. Stop after
  one legible comparison and its limits are recorded.
- **What it requires:** Two separated fresh contexts or external readers, the
  frozen text and target, exact prompts, and an orchestrator verification pass.
- **Execution placement:** **Hybrid, with a sequential fresh-context handoff.**
  The summary reader sees only the text and summary prompt. After the usability
  check, the close reader sees only the text, usable summary, and neutral
  comparison prompt. The orchestrator verifies cited differences and returns
  the bounded reading. Without two separated readers, downgrade to an author
  compression check and do not call it this assay.
