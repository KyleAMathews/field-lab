# Evidence-to-claim chart (`evidence-to-claim`)

- **Phenomenon sought:** The relational structure by which a bounded body of evidence supports, attacks, qualifies, or fails to reach one factual proposition or time-indexed forecast.
- **Why use it:** Research notes and strategy memos often mix source evidence, testimony, assumptions, generalizations, and conclusions in prose. The chart makes each inferential step, rival route, and unsupported bridge inspectable without pretending to calculate belief.
- **Operating range:** Use for due diligence, incident conclusions, historical or investigative questions, technical claims, and other factual inquiries with a stable target proposition. An exact forecast may run only as a **forecast-support audit**: freeze its horizon, population, observable terms, and truth conditions; keep terminal evidence in the present or past; type every forward bridge as a generalization or assumption; and do not infer probability, confidence, or acceptance. Do not use to map chronology, generate options, settle values, or chart an inquiry whose target changes faster than the record can be revised.
- **Input:** One exact ultimate factual proposition or time-indexed forecast; the analyst's role, inquiry stage, purpose, accessible data, time, resource, and access constraints; a bounded source corpus; and the strongest live proponent and opponent accounts.
- **What changes:** The instrument decomposes prose into numbered propositions and explicit relation types. This exposes assumptions and gaps but can strip temporal, narrative, institutional, or social meaning from the record.
- **Procedure:**
  1. **Freeze standpoint and target.** Record analyst role, inquiry stage, available and obtainable data, purpose, constraints, and the exact ultimate proposition. A material wording change starts a new chart version because it may change relevance. In a fast-moving domain, also freeze a temporal-generation register: observation window, source cutoff, population, system or model generation, harness or workflow, and the date through which each capability claim is current. Any extension beyond that register must become a visible inferential bridge.
  2. **Decompose the target.** State penultimate propositions and whether each is individually necessary, jointly sufficient, or merely contributory. Preserve unresolved logical structure rather than choosing a convenient decomposition. When a strategic or investigative story contains several ultimate claims, split it into a linked-chart campaign. Share one corpus register, keep one exact ultimate proposition per chart, and record cross-chart dependencies. An earlier chart's conclusion may enter a later chart only as a typed inferred proposition with a pointer to its chart and version; it does not become terminal evidence.
  3. **Separate accounts.** Record the proponent's assertion, contextual factual story, and case theory. Record opponent responses separately as explanation, denial, or rival hypothesis; an opponent need not prove a complete alternative to block the proponent's route.
  4. **Build the proposition key.** Atomize material claims and assign stable identifiers. Type testimonial assertions separately from inferred facts. Type terminal support as source or physical evidence, testimony, explicit generalization, explicit assumption, or judicially noticed/accepted background fact where the domain permits it. Keep source pointers and provenance attacks with the proposition they qualify. Group nodes derived from one source family and mark derivative or non-independent statements so atomization cannot make one source look like several corroborating sources.
  5. **Chart relations.** Draw support, attack, explanation, denial, and rival-hypothesis links. Mark conjunctive, disjunctive, and corroborative support. Use a master chart with nested subcharts when needed; do not force the whole inquiry onto one page.
  6. **Synchronize and test.** Require one-to-one correspondence between chart and key. Read every support link downward as “because” and upward as “therefore.” Redraft propositions or relations when either direction fails. Do not use “and then” as inferential support.
  7. **Inspect the architecture.** Mark free-floating claims, missing terminal support, weak or unstated generalizations, circular support, inconsistent rivals, credibility/authenticity/provenance attacks, temporal overreach, source-family non-independence, and evidence that could discriminate among live paths. List the unsupported extensions that a reader might otherwise carry beyond the frozen proposition.
  8. **Return without weighing.** Report the chart, key, rival paths, and gaps. Do not infer that more nodes or corroborating lines mean greater weight, calculate a probability, or decide which belief should be accepted unless the user separately asks for that judgment. If a shared Field Log schema requires a reading-confidence label, apply it only to the fidelity of the saved source or trace and state separately that acceptance of the ultimate proposition was **not weighed**.
- **Result:** Return the frozen standpoint and ultimate proposition; temporal-generation register when relevant; penultimate decomposition; account register; shared corpus and cross-chart dependency ledger when several charts form one campaign; numbered proposition key; chart or adjacency representation; typed nodes and relations; source-family and non-independence register; unsupported extensions; attacked links; discriminating evidence gaps; revision history; claim-acceptance status `not weighed`; and stopping basis.
- **Control:** Exact target wording controls relevance drift. Chart-key synchronization and bidirectional “because/therefore” checks control link quality. Rival accounts, terminal-support typing, a temporal-generation register, and source-family grouping expose one-sided proof, stale extensions, and false corroboration. Cross-chart typing prevents one audited conclusion from silently becoming evidence for the next. A second reader may audit a bounded subchart, but their disagreement is another chart judgment, not ground truth.
- **Common distortions:** The analyst chooses a target that bakes in the conclusion; atomization removes qualifying context; one source is split into many nodes and appears overweighted; several product primitives look like one coherent system; an older observation rides untyped into a newer capability generation; an earlier chart's conclusion becomes evidence by repetition; chronology becomes support; or a polished graph looks like a verdict.
- **Escalate / stop:** Use [`substrate-map`](substrate-map.md) first when the material is still a mixed chronology. Use [`belief-stress`](belief-stress.md) when rival positions need separated committed advocacy. Stop when the target cannot be frozen, the corpus boundary is unknown, or charting would require domain judgments no available analyst can make.
- **What it requires:** High model effort, a bounded source corpus, and iterative analyst review. For a consequential claim, preserve the exact chart, key, source version, and standpoint with any later decision that cites it.
- **Execution placement:** **Orchestrator.** It must keep the user's exact target, evolving corrections, source boundary, and proposition key synchronized. A fresh agent may audit one frozen subchart while seeing only that subchart and its sources. If the corpus is too large, delegate source-grounded proposition extraction but let the orchestrator own relevance, relation typing, and the return.
- **Distinctness:** Unlike [`belief-stress`](belief-stress.md), this instrument maps one corpus to one exact truth-apt proposition rather than eliciting full-strength advocacy. Unlike [`structural-recombine`](structural-recombine.md), it charts inferential support rather than proposing cross-source designs. Unlike [`substrate-map`](substrate-map.md), it maps logic rather than sequence. It may supply grounded propositions to those instruments without replacing their operations.
- **Provenance:** This Field Lab port adapts Modified Wigmorean Analysis from Paul Roberts and Colin Aitken's [*The Logic of Forensic Proof*](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~cgga/Guide-3-WEB.pdf), with scope limits informed by Peter Tillers and David Schum's [“Charting New Territory in Judicial Proof”](https://larc.cardozo.yu.edu/clr/vol9/iss3/2/). It generalizes the relational audit outside law while dropping legal proof standards unless supplied by the chosen domain.
