# Position-preservation assay (`position-preservation`)

- **Phenomenon / range / input:** Whether a candidate preserves a position's real insight while changing its frame; needs the original position and one candidate.
- **Why use it:** The author of a synthesis cannot reliably judge what it erased. A committed validator shows what the result preserved or defeated from inside the source position.
- **Perturbation / procedure:** Give the original or freshly reconstructed committed agent its position, evidence, success rule, and only one candidate. Ask it to identify the position's necessary insight, show exactly where the candidate preserves or defeats it, test whether the candidate raises the insight into a stronger frame rather than restating or compromising it, and request the smallest repair. Keep sibling candidates and the author's reasoning hidden.
- **Readout / control:** Preserved insight, defeated claim, elevation test, and requested repair; keep each validator blind to sibling candidates.
- **Common artifacts:** A Monk protects rhetoric, identity, or total victory rather than the insight that made its position necessary.
- **Escalate / stop:** Escalate when a repair can preserve more without reverting to compromise. Stop when the candidate clearly passes, fails, or needs new substrate.
- **What it requires:** One agent per position per candidate; keep it with the validation trace.
- **Execution placement:** **Fresh or resumed committed subagent required.** Each validator sees its original position and only the candidate under test, never sibling candidates or orchestrator synthesis reasoning. Commitment and separation create the preservation test. If no suitable agent is available, use a lower-confidence trace audit and do not claim position validation. Return each verdict and requested repair intact; later phase analysis may interpret them separately.
