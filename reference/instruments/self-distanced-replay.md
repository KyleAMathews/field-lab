# Self-distanced replay (`self-distanced-replay`)

- **Phenomenon sought:** Features of one event that become easier for the user to notice when their own account is rendered from a modest observer distance.
- **Why use it:** First-person retelling can keep the original pace and viewpoint fixed. A careful observer-view replay can alter narrative distance while preserving the account, making actions, reported experience, interpretations, and gaps easier to see separately.
- **Operating range:** Use only on a user-owned, bounded, non-crisis event when the user wants a different rendering rather than an explanation. Do not use when distance itself feels unsafe or invalidating, when the user wants direct emotional expression, or when the source is mainly about another person's hidden motives.
- **Input:** One source account, the user's preferred name or third-person reference, and permission to recast it. Do not require disclosure beyond the supplied account.
- **What changes:** The model changes grammatical person, vantage, and narrative spacing. Those changes can reduce felt immediacy, but they can also flatten meaning or imply a false neutral observer.
- **Procedure:**
  1. Freeze the source account and the user's terms for the people involved.
  2. Extract four source-linked registers: visible or reported action, reported thoughts and feelings, the user's interpretations, and missing information.
  3. Render a short replay from the viewpoint of a respectful observer who knows only the frozen account. Use the user's name or chosen third-person form. Preserve uncertainty and emotional language; do not add motives, diagnoses, advice, or a lesson.
  4. Place any material that cannot survive the perspective shift in a separate “not visible from here” note rather than deleting it.
  5. Return the replay beside a compact source trace showing changed wording and omissions.
  6. Ask one open question: “What, if anything, feels clearer or wrong in this rendering?” Record the correction in the user's own words and stop before analysis.
- **Result:** Return the replay, its source trace, the four registers, omitted or unrenderable material, and the user's correction. Treat any added ease or insight as user testimony, not a measured effect.
- **Control:** Keep the original account available. Every factual clause in the replay must trace to it. Compare against an ordinary summary during formal testing, not every use. The user decides whether the distance helped.
- **Common distortions:** Third person sounds clinical; “observer” implies truth; feeling is stripped from the account; interpretive claims become visible facts; gaps are filled; or distance is mistaken for disbelief or emotional health.
- **Escalate / stop:** Stop when the user says the replay feels alienating, flattening, or too intense. Another instrument or any analysis needs a separate choice.
- **What it requires:** One short transformation and one correction exchange. Use the user's existing account; do not turn it into another interview.
- **Execution placement:** **Orchestrator.** It needs the exact source, the user's naming choice, and live correction. A fresh agent adds no needed independence and may increase source drift.
- **Distinctness:** Unlike a summary, the operation deliberately changes perspective while preserving source registers. Unlike [`defamiliarize`](defamiliarize.md), it does not use metaphor or a distant practice. Unlike [`behavior-chain`](behavior-chain.md), it does not propose functions or causal links.
- **Provenance:** Experimental self-distancing research studies changes in perspective during reflection; this LLM-authored replay is a new transfer, not the tested intervention. See a [meta-analysis of 25 experiments](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/smi.3199) and a [review covering 48 studies](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02699931.2022.2134094).
