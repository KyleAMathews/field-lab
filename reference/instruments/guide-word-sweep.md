# Guide-word deviation sweep (`guide-word-sweep`)

- **Phenomenon sought:** Credible ways a declared system or procedure can deviate from intent, together with their causes, consequences, safeguards, unresolved information, corrective actions, and revalidation triggers.
- **Why use it:** Teams usually inspect familiar failures. A fixed guide-word vocabulary forces consistent challenge across every relevant node and leaves a coverage and closure record that can be revisited after the system changes.
- **Operating range:** Use for operational processes, procedures, launches, protocols, interfaces, agent workflows, and other systems with stable intent and decomposable nodes. Do not use as a likelihood estimator, a substitute for domain expertise, or a coverage claim for tightly coupled multi-failure software or electronic behavior that cannot be represented as bounded deviations.
- **Input:** Study purpose and scope; declared design intent; current diagrams, procedures, incident history, assumptions, and limits; bounded nodes or steps; relevant parameters; a study leader; competent content reviewers; and an owner for follow-up actions.
- **What changes:** The instrument applies a predefined perturbation vocabulary to a system description. It creates deviations the source never asserted, so credibility, consequence, and safeguard judgments remain review work rather than model output.
- **Procedure:**
  1. **Define the study.** Freeze scope, purpose, design intent, lifecycle stage, success and safe-limit assumptions, exclusions, information quality, team roles, and action authority. Record relevant prior incidents and adjacent systems or controls.
  2. **Prepare nodes and parameters.** Divide the system into reviewable nodes or procedural steps without hiding cross-node controls. For each, list relevant parameters such as presence, amount, rate, timing, order, direction, composition, authorization, destination, frequency, or duration. Mark injected parameters.
  3. **Run the fixed sweep.** Apply `No`, `More`, `Less`, `Part of`, `As well as`, `Reverse`, and `Other than` to each relevant parameter. Record every considered pair as credible, information-needed, not credible with reason, or not applicable. Do not replace the fixed sweep with free brainstorming; add separately labeled domain guide words only when their provenance is clear.
  4. **Trace each credible deviation.** Record initiating and enabling causes; immediate and downstream consequences; affected people, systems, or environments; existing preventive, detective, and mitigative safeguards; interactions among controls; consequences of safeguard failure; and available warning signs.
  5. **Challenge the record.** The leader asks for assumptions, hidden common causes, human factors, facility or context effects, and recovery limits. Keep uncertainty and information gaps. Do not assign likelihood unless a separately chosen method supplies it.
  6. **Create actions.** For material gaps or inadequate safeguards, record a recommendation, owner, due date, affected parties, and completion evidence. A rejection must keep its reason, such as factual error, lack of necessity, an adequate alternative, or infeasibility under the declared standard.
  7. **Close and communicate.** Verify completion evidence, residual gaps, and communication to affected operators or maintainers. Closure means the recorded action was dispositioned, not that the deviation became impossible.
  8. **Set revalidation.** Record a review date and change triggers such as altered components, materials, models, procedures, limits, dependencies, users, or neighboring processes. On waking, reopen only affected nodes and shared safeguards, retain the earlier version, and state what changed.
- **Result:** Return the frozen study definition; node-parameter matrix; complete guide-word coverage ledger; credible deviations; cause-consequence-safeguard traces; control-failure and human-factor notes; information gaps; action owners and dispositions; closure evidence; version; and scheduled or event-triggered revalidation rules. Do not rank by risk without an authorized likelihood or value model.
- **Control:** The fixed coverage matrix exposes skipped combinations. Current design records and incident history bound the baseline. Multidisciplinary challenge reduces one-person blind spots. Assumption, information-gap, action, and version ledgers prevent silent closure. The single-deviation boundary remains explicit; coupled failures require a different method.
- **Common distortions:** The LLM generates impressive but physically meaningless deviations; every row looks equally important; node decomposition hides shared controls; safeguards are assumed independent; action closure becomes paperwork; periodic waking repeats stale analysis; or the study claims probabilistic risk reduction it never measured.
- **Escalate / stop:** Give one consequential deviation to [`hostile-assay`](hostile-assay.md) for a fresh failure test. Use [`ground-condition`](ground-condition.md) when a deviation depends on a material boundary. Stop when intent is unstable, records are inaccurate, competent review is unavailable, nodes cannot isolate meaningful deviations, or coupled multi-failure behavior contains the main risk. When competent review is unavailable, return only a preparation artifact: the frozen study definition, draft node-parameter matrix, fixed guide-word worksheet, missing reviewer roles, and questions those reviewers must resolve. Record the run as stopped or prepared, not completed; generated deviations, safeguard judgments, actions, and closure claims are not a reading.
- **What it requires:** High model and reviewer effort, a competent leader, current system records, and persistent ownership of follow-up. An LLM may enumerate guide-word pairs, retrieve source material, maintain traceability, and prepare revalidation queues; it does not decide credibility, consequence, adequacy, or closure.
- **Execution placement:** **Hybrid.** The orchestrator owns scope, user corrections, the coverage ledger, and the final return. A bounded generation worker may expand the frozen node-parameter matrix without seeing preferred failures. Domain reviewers own credibility, consequences, safeguards, and actions. Without those reviewers, the worker may prepare the study and worksheet but may not run or complete the sweep.
- **Distinctness:** Unlike [`attribute-interpolation`](attribute-interpolation.md), this instrument applies a fixed anomaly vocabulary across every relevant node and follows each credible deviation into consequences and closure. Unlike [`hostile-assay`](hostile-assay.md), it seeks systematic coverage rather than a blind attack on one candidate. [`design-grammar`](design-grammar.md) may supply grounded intent, components, interfaces, and invariants; this instrument challenges their operational deviations and maintains the follow-up record.
- **Provenance:** This Field Lab port adapts HAZOP from [IEC 61882:2016](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/24321) and the United States Environmental Protection Agency's [Risk Management Program Guidance for Chemical Distributors](https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=P100E9E3.TXT). The generalized parameter vocabulary, LLM role, and recurring observatory behavior are Field Lab adaptations and must not be mistaken for IEC certification or regulatory compliance.
