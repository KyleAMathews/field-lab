# Stake map (`stake-map`)

- **Phenomenon sought:** What people protect, need, fear, value, or cannot change.
- **Why use it:** Proposed solutions hide the burdens they answer. Structured questions separate reported stakes, inferred stakes, and fixed constraints by person.
- **Operating range:** Coordination, preferences, personal choices, norms, and relationship friction. Do not use to diagnose a person or invent hidden motives.
- **Input:** The affected people and one concrete point of friction.
- **Perturbation:** Direct attention from stated solutions to the cost each person experiences.
- **Procedure:** For each person, distinguish feelings, desired outcome, standard, constraint, feared loss, and priority. Ask for missing testimony rather than fill it in.
- **Readout:** A compact map of aligned stakes, conflicting stakes, and unknown stakes.
- **Control:** Mark user testimony separately from inference; offer inferred stakes as questions.
- **Common artifact:** The orchestrator supplies a polished motive the person never endorsed.
- **Escalate when:** Two valid stakes cannot fit together, or a repeated word hides different standards.
- **Stop when:** Each person can recognize the map and, if action would help, a safe Real-world check is available with agreement from everyone affected.
- **What it requires:** A short conversation and correction from the people whose stakes are being mapped.
- **Execution placement:** **Orchestrator.** It keeps direct access to the user's testimony and marks inferred stakes as questions. A subagent may re-scan an existing transcript only as a separate control, not replace elicitation. If live testimony is unavailable, return a provisional map with gaps. The orchestrator presents it for recognition or correction.
