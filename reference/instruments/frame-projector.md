# Frame Projector (`frame-projector`)

- **Phenomenon sought:** Dominant clusters in ambiguous, high-dimensional phenomenology; candidate separating lines; evocative prototypes; and missing quadrants.
- **Unaided limit:** Starting from favored axes hides whether the boxes describe real clusters.
- **Access differential:** Rao's cluster-first projection makes the phenomenology, candidate groupings, separating lines, naming choices, occupied cells, and empty cells inspectable in sequence.
- **Operating range:** Use when a bounded question or territory contains enough observed, sourced, elicited, or explicitly generated items to cluster. Avoid when the material is plainly one-dimensional, statistically tractable, clear from first principles, or better represented as a taxonomy.
- **Input:** A bounded question or territory and a typed inventory of the material to project.
- **Perturbation:** Cluster the material before proposing axes, then use separating lines and evocative labels to test several possible maps.

## Execution placement

- **Execution seat:** **Either; orchestrator by default.** Use fresh subagents for several decorrelated projections; if unavailable, the orchestrator generates several candidates.
- **Context boundary:** The executor sees the specimen and candidate distinctions but not a preferred winning frame.
- **Placement rationale:** Fresh contexts can widen the candidate projections, but separation does not create the instrument's basic access.
- **Fallback:** If fresh agents are unavailable, generate several candidates in the orchestrator and state the correlation risk.
- **Return path:** Return all controlled maps without selecting one for the user.

## Procedure

Follow the full [Rao sequence](../quadrant-diagrams.md#rao-sequence):

1. Inventory and type the phenomenology.
2. Form candidate four-cluster partitions before proposing axes.
3. Discover separating lines and record their chemistry and failures.
4. Workshop evocative labels in the appropriate naming mode.
5. Render and repopulate every candidate diagram.
6. Reject or revise maps that fail the examples, chemistry, or independence checks.

Generate several honest candidates without choosing a winning frame. Preserve a contraindication when no candidate earns a second axis.

## Readout

Return these blocks in order:

1. Phenomenology inventory
2. Candidate clusters and members
3. Candidate separators and failures
4. Label workshop
5. Rendered maps
6. Calibration

Render every candidate as a complete inline ASCII or box-drawing 2×2 under [the rendering contract](../quadrant-diagrams.md#rendering-contract). Show both named axes and poles, visible directions, four bounded labeled cells, one or two relevant examples in every occupied cell or an explicit empty mark, source pointers, axis claim type, second-axis confidence, and the axis-independence result.

A polished diagram without its formation trace—or a one-line `axis × axis: four labels` list, bullets, or table—is incomplete.

## Calibration and controls

- Keep observed, sourced, elicited, and generated items distinct.
- Test axis independence instead of treating two important-sounding terms as orthogonal.
- Repopulate each map from the inventory; do not invent examples merely to fill cells.
- State whether each axis is analytic, empirical, conceptual, or artistic.
- State source limits, confidence in the second axis, and what remains unmeasured.

## Common artifacts

- Axes chosen before looking at the phenomenology
- Clusters reverse-engineered to fill boxes
- False orthogonality or symmetry
- Witty labels detached from their members
- Generated examples mistaken for evidence
- An empty quadrant mistaken for a solution

## Escalation and stop conditions

- **Escalate** when a missing quadrant has evidence or a constituency.
- **Stop** when one map yields a useful next probe or no candidate earns its second axis.
- **Do not run** when the material is plainly one-dimensional; record the contraindication instead.

## Cost and persistence

One to three turns. Keep the readout in the session unless maps must be compared or resumed.
