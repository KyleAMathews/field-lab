# Tension statement (`tension-statement`)

- **Phenomenon sought:** The smallest contradiction that explains recurring friction.
- **Why use it:** A list of complaints does not show which demands collide under which condition. Compression makes one candidate mechanism testable.
- **Operating range:** Two or more sound demands that cannot all hold under current conditions. Do not force a dialectic when evidence favors one side.
- **Input:** At least two claims or stakes and the condition that makes them collide.
- **Perturbation:** Compresses a messy field into a candidate structure.
- **Procedure:** State: “We need or value **A**, and we also need or value **B**, but under **C**, doing A undermines B because **mechanism**.” Then search for omitted facts, actors, and axes.
- **Readout:** One or two candidate tensions, each with disconfirming evidence that would weaken it.
- **Control:** Restate the specimen without the binary and see what disappears.
- **Common artifact:** The syntax manufactures symmetry or conflict.
- **Escalate when:** Both poles remain live after correction and the outcome matters.
- **Stop when:** One or two candidate tensions cover the reported friction and their disconfirming observations are clear.
- **What it requires:** A brief conversation and enough concrete examples to test the wording.
- **Execution placement:** **Orchestrator.** It needs the accumulated claims, stakes, and user corrections to compress the live contradiction without inventing one. A fresh subagent may later challenge the statement but does not own the first compression. If continuity is lost, restate the substrate and ask the user to confirm it. The orchestrator presents the candidate tension.
